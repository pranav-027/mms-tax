## **SECTION A: THEORY NOTES**

---

### **1. INTRODUCTION TO TAXATION**

Taxation is a compulsory financial charge imposed by the government on individuals or entities to fund public expenditure. Taxes in India are broadly classified into two categories:

#### **A. Direct Tax**
- **Definition:** When the **incidence** (liability) and **impact** (burden) of tax fall on the **same person**, it is called a Direct Tax.
- The assessee himself bears the tax and feels the financial pinch.
- **Nature:** *Progressive* — the rate increases with an increase in income.
- **Examples:**
  - Income Tax (Income Tax Act, 1961)
  - Wealth Tax (Wealth Tax Act, 1957)
  - Gift Tax (now abolished)

#### **B. Indirect Tax**
- **Definition:** When the **incidence** and **impact** fall on **two different persons**, it is called an Indirect Tax.
- The tax is collected from one person (seller) but the burden is shifted to another (consumer).
- **Nature:** *Regressive* — affects all consumers equally regardless of income.
- **Examples:**
  - Goods and Services Tax (GST Act, 2017)
  - Customs Duty (Customs Act, 1962)

---

### **2. SCOPE OF THE INCOME TAX ACT, 1961**

The Act covers **Sections 1 to 80**, dealing with:
1. **Basic concepts** — meaning of income, taxable persons, taxable period, computation methods.
2. **Computation of taxable income** under five heads:
   - Income from Salaries
   - Income from House Property
   - Profits and Gains of Business or Profession
   - Capital Gains
   - Income from Other Sources
3. **Deductions from total income** — savings (Sec 80C), disability (80U), mediclaim (80D), pension contribution (80CCD), etc.

---

### **3. SCHEME OF THE ACT**

- The Act is divided into **Chapters → Sections → Sub-sections → Clauses → Sub-clauses**.
- Each provides:
  - Explanation of provisions
  - Conditions for applicability
  - Exceptions, if any
- Amendments are made annually through the **Finance Act (Budget)** passed by Parliament.

---

### **4. ASSESSMENT YEAR – Section 2(9)**

- **Definition:** A period of **12 months** commencing on the **1st day of April** every year and ending on **31st March** of the next year.
- **Example:** Assessment Year (A.Y.) **2024-25** = 1st April 2024 to 31st March 2025.
- It is the year in which the income earned in the previous year is **assessed and taxed**.

---

### **5. PREVIOUS YEAR – Section 3**

- **Definition:** The financial year **immediately preceding** the assessment year.
- Income earned in the Previous Year (P.Y.) is taxed in the next year, i.e., the Assessment Year (A.Y.).

#### **Special Case – Newly Set-up Business/Profession:**
- The Previous Year shall begin from the **date of setting up** of the business/profession and end on **31st March** of the same financial year.
- **Example:** If business is set up on 15th September 2024:
  - P.Y. = 15th Sept 2024 to 31st March 2025
  - A.Y. = 2025-26
  - From the next year onwards, full financial year (1st April – 31st March) will apply.

---

### **6. FINANCIAL YEAR**

- As per **Section 3(21) of the General Clauses Act, 1897**, a Financial Year is a period of 12 months from **1st April to 31st March**.
- It plays a **dual role**:
  - Acts as **Assessment Year** for the preceding previous year.
  - Acts as **Previous Year** for the succeeding assessment year.
- **Example:** Financial Year 2024-25 is:
  - A.Y. for P.Y. 2023-24
  - P.Y. for A.Y. 2025-26

---

### **7. PERSON – Section 2(31)**

A "Person" includes the following **seven categories**:

| **Sr. No.** | **Type of Person** | **Description** |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Individual** | A natural human being (male, female, minor, lunatic) |
| 2 | **Hindu Undivided Family (HUF)** | Persons lineally descended from a common Hindu ancestor including wives & unmarried daughters |
| 3 | **Company** | Indian company, foreign company, or any entity declared as a company by CBDT |
| 4 | **Firm** | Partnership Firm or LLP under LLP Act, 2008 |
| 5 | **Association of Persons (AOP)** | Persons joining for a common purpose |
| 6 | **Body of Individuals (BOI)** | Group of individuals (e.g., a club, cooperative society) |
| 7 | **Local Authority** | Municipality, District Board, Port Commissioners |
| 8 | **Artificial Juridical Person** | Entities under special Act – LIC, University, Bar Council |

---

### **8. ASSESSEE vs. PERSON**

- An **Assessee** is a person liable to pay tax or any sum under the Act.
- The taxpayer may be **different** from the income earner:
  - Legal representative pays tax on a deceased person's income.
  - Guardian pays tax for a minor child.
  - Employer is liable if TDS is not deducted from salary.

---

### **9. INCOME – Section 2(24)**

Income is an **inclusive definition**. It includes:

1. Profits and Gains
2. Dividends
3. Voluntary contributions to charitable/religious trusts
4. Value of perquisites or profits in lieu of salary
5. Special allowances/benefits to meet personal expenses
6. Benefit from a company by a director or substantial shareholder
7. Benefit to representative assessee or beneficiary
8. Fair Market Value of inventory
9. Capital Gains
10. Profits of mutual insurance companies / cooperative societies (insurance & banking)
11. Winnings from lotteries, crosswords, races, card games, gambling, betting
12. Employee contributions received by employer for welfare funds
13. Sum received under **Keyman Insurance Policy** (including bonus)
14. **Gifts from non-relatives** (above prescribed limit)
15. Excess consideration on issue of shares above FMV
16. Subsidies, grants, incentives, duty drawbacks (other than specified ones)

#### **Other Notes:**
- Includes negative income, disputed income, reimbursement, double taxation.
- **Pin Money** (small amounts received by wife from husband for household savings) is **NOT income**.
- **Awards** = incidental income.
- **Embezzled money** = taxable (Act doesn't differentiate between legal/illegal income).

---

### **10. RATE OF TAX (A.Y. 2026-27)**

#### **(A) NEW TAX REGIME (DEFAULT)**

- **Default option** for A.Y. 2026-27.
- Lower tax rates with **higher exemption limit**.
- Most deductions/exemptions (80C, HRA, etc.) **NOT available**.

**Key Highlights:**
| **Feature** | **Benefit** |
|---|---|
| Basic Exemption Limit | ₹4,00,000 (uniform for all individuals & HUFs) |
| Tax Rebate u/s 87A | Income up to ₹12,00,000 effectively tax-free |
| Standard Deduction | ₹75,000 (for salaried individuals) |
| Effective Tax-Free Limit (Salaried) | ₹12,75,000 |

#### **(B) OLD TAX REGIME (OPTIONAL)**

- Beneficial for taxpayers with **significant deductions** (80C, HRA, home loan interest).
- Basic exemption limits vary based on **age** (Senior, Super Senior, etc.).

---

### **11. REBATE UNDER SECTION 87A**

- Tax benefit for **resident individuals** with low-to-middle income.
- Reduces total tax liability **before health & education cess (4%)**.

| **Regime** | **Income Limit** | **Maximum Rebate** |
|---|---|---|
| **New Regime** | Up to ₹12,00,000 | ₹60,000 |
| **Old Regime** | Up to ₹5,00,000 | ₹12,500 |

**Eligibility Criteria:**
- Only **Resident Individuals** are eligible.
- Rebate ≤ Total Tax Payable (before cess).
- **NOT applicable** on Long-Term Capital Gains under Section 112A.

---

### **12. DIFFERENCE: PREVIOUS YEAR vs. ASSESSMENT YEAR**

| **Basis** | **Previous Year** | **Assessment Year** |
|---|---|---|
| Meaning | Year in which income is earned | Year in which income is taxed |
| Section | Sec 3 | Sec 2(9) |
| Duration | 12 months (or less in case of new business) | Always 12 months |
| Example | 2023-24 | 2024-25 |

## **SECTION B: NUMERICAL QUESTIONS WITH SOLUTIONS**

---

### **Numerical 1: Determining Previous Year**

**Q.** Anmol started a new business on 1st June 2023. What will be the Previous Year and Assessment Year?

**Solution:**
- **Previous Year:** 1st June 2023 to 31st March 2024 (since business is newly set up)
- **Assessment Year:** 2024-25
- From the next year onwards, P.Y. will be a full financial year (1st April – 31st March).

---

### **Numerical 2: Determining F.Y., P.Y., and A.Y.**

**Q.** Mr. Akash, previously unemployed, gets a job on 1st August 2022. Find the F.Y., P.Y., and A.Y.

**Solution:**
- **Financial Year:** 2022-23
- **Previous Year:** 1st August 2022 to 31st March 2023 (for new source of income — salary)
- **Assessment Year:** 2023-24

---

### **Numerical 3: Tax Liability under New Regime**

**Q.** Mr. Ramu has salary income of ₹12,00,000. He invested ₹1,50,000 in PPF and ₹30,000 in health insurance. Calculate tax liability for FY 2025-26.

**Solution (New Regime):**
- Gross Income = ₹12,00,000
- Less: Standard Deduction = ₹75,000
- Taxable Income = ₹11,25,000
- Since income ≤ ₹12,00,000 → Eligible for **Rebate u/s 87A (₹60,000)**
- **Net Tax Payable = NIL**

*(Under Old Regime, tax liability would be ₹1,10,760 — hence New Regime is more beneficial.)*

---

### **Numerical 4: Comparison of Old vs. New Regime**

**Q.** Mr. Anban has salary of ₹25,00,000. Pays rent ₹45,000/month, claims HRA ₹4,00,000, has 80C ₹1,50,000, 80D ₹50,000, and 80CCD(1B) ₹50,000. Compare tax under both regimes.

**Solution:**

| **Particulars** | **Amount (₹)** |
|---|---|
| New Regime Tax Liability | 3,19,800 |
| Old Regime Tax Liability | 3,04,200 |
| **Tax Saved (Old Regime)** | **15,600** |

**Conclusion:** Mr. Anban should choose the **Old Regime** as he has total deductions worth ₹9,00,000.

---

### **Numerical 5: Choice of Regime with Minimal Deductions**

**Q.** Mr. K has salary ₹20,00,000 and FD interest ₹20,000 (no deductions). Compare tax under both regimes.

**Solution:**

| **Particulars** | **Amount (₹)** |
|---|---|
| Tax under Old Regime (incl. cess) | 4,19,640 |
| Tax under New Regime (incl. cess) | 1,96,560 |
| **Tax Saved (New Regime)** | **2,23,080** |

**Conclusion:** Without deductions, the **New Regime** is far more beneficial.
