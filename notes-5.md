# **UNIT 1: IMPACT OF DIRECT TAXES ON FINANCIAL & OPERATIONAL DECISIONS**

---

### **1. INTRODUCTION TO TAXATION**

Taxation is an **essential part of any economy** and plays a critical role in **government revenue collection**. It represents the compulsory financial contribution made by individuals and entities to the government to enable it to perform its sovereign functions such as defense, administration, infrastructure, and welfare programs.

In India, businesses are subject to two broad categories of taxes:

#### **(A) Direct Taxes:**
- Imposed directly on the **income or wealth** of individuals and corporations.
- The **incidence (legal liability)** and **impact (economic burden)** fall on the **same person**.
- Cannot be shifted to another person.
- Examples: Income Tax, Corporate Tax, Capital Gains Tax, MAT, STT, Wealth Tax.

#### **(B) Indirect Taxes:**
- Levied on the **consumption of goods and services**.
- The burden of tax can be **shifted** from one person to another (seller → consumer).
- Examples: GST, Customs Duty.

---

### **2. SIGNIFICANCE OF DIRECT TAXES IN BUSINESS DECISIONS**

Direct taxes are **critical determinants** in corporate financial and operational decision-making because:

1. They directly affect **profitability** of the business.
2. **High tax rates** can stifle growth and investment.
3. **Targeted tax incentives** can drive innovation, investment, and strategic operational choices.
4. They influence decisions like **location selection, capital structure, and investment timing**.
5. They determine **distribution policies** (dividends vs. reinvestment).

---

### **3. IMPACT OF DIRECT TAXES ON FINANCIAL DECISIONS**

Financial decisions are decisions related to **funding, investment, and distribution** of profits. Direct taxes significantly influence each of these areas.

---

#### **(A) CAPITAL STRUCTURE: DEBT vs. EQUITY**

**Concept:**
Capital structure refers to the mix of **debt and equity** used by a company to finance its operations and growth.

**Tax Impact:**
- The **interest paid on debt** is treated as a **deductible expense** under the Income Tax Act, thereby reducing the taxable profits of the company.
- This phenomenon is known as the **"Tax Shield"** of debt.
- In contrast, **dividends paid on equity** are NOT deductible.

**Implication:**
- Companies often **prefer debt over equity** to take advantage of the tax shield.
- This results in **lower effective cost of capital**.

**Example:**
If a company earns ₹10,00,000 and pays ₹2,00,000 as interest:
- Without debt: Tax @ 30% on ₹10,00,000 = ₹3,00,000
- With debt: Tax @ 30% on (₹10,00,000 – ₹2,00,000) = ₹2,40,000
- **Tax Saved (Tax Shield) = ₹60,000**

**Limitations:**
- Excessive debt increases **financial risk** (default risk).
- Vulnerable to **interest rate fluctuations**.
- Higher debt may **lower credit rating**.
- Financial managers must **balance tax savings vs. financial stability**.

---

#### **(B) DIVIDEND POLICY**

**Concept:**
Dividend policy determines whether a company should **distribute profits** to shareholders or **retain them** for reinvestment.

**Tax Impact:**
- Earlier (Pre-2020 in India): **Dividend Distribution Tax (DDT)** was levied on companies.
- After **April 2020**: Dividends are **taxable in the hands of shareholders** at applicable slab rates.
- TDS @ 10% applies if dividend exceeds ₹5,000 (₹10,000 from FY 2025-26).

**Implication:**
- Higher dividend tax → companies prefer **retaining profits**.
- Lower dividend tax → companies pay more dividends.
- Recent trend in India shows **dividend cuts** as companies prioritize **cash retention** for growth.

**Operational Decisions Influenced:**
- Reinvestment vs. distribution.
- Bonus shares vs. cash dividends.
- Buy-back of shares (taxed differently).

---

#### **(C) INVESTMENT AND CAPITAL ALLOCATION**

**Concept:**
Investment decisions deal with **where and how much capital** to invest in long-term assets.

**Tax Impact:**
- **Corporate Income Tax** directly reduces **net profitability** and the **Return on Investment (ROI)**.
- **Capital Gains Tax** affects timing of asset sales.
- **High taxes** reduce retained earnings available for expansion.
- **Tax incentives** like **accelerated depreciation, R&D deductions, SEZ benefits** encourage investment in priority sectors.

**Example:**
If a company invests in **R&D**, it can claim **150%–200% deduction** under Section 35 (in specified cases), thereby reducing taxable income and encouraging innovation.

**Strategic Implications:**
- Investment in **renewable energy, manufacturing, startups** often promoted via tax holidays.
- Tax-favored sectors attract more capital inflow.

---

#### **(D) CASH FLOW MANAGEMENT**

**Concept:**
Cash flow management involves planning the **timing of inflows and outflows** to maintain liquidity.

**Tax Impact:**
- **Advance Tax payments** (in 4 installments: 15th June, 15th Sept, 15th Dec, 15th March) reduce available cash.
- **TDS deductions** affect working capital.
- **GST refunds** and **income tax refunds** are part of cash flow planning.

**Implication:**
- Companies must plan **tax payments** to avoid liquidity crunches.
- Effective tax planning improves **working capital efficiency**.

---

#### **(E) RESERVES AND REINVESTMENT MANAGEMENT**

**Concept:**
Reserves represent retained profits used for **future expansion, R&D, or new ventures**.

**Tax Impact:**
- Tax credits and deductions for **reinvestment** encourage growth.
- **Specific tax incentives** for investment in:
  - New product development
  - Geographical expansion
  - Skill development
  - Infrastructure projects

**Example:**
A company reinvesting ₹50 lakh in eligible R&D may claim weighted deduction, reducing tax liability and freeing funds for further investment.

---

### **4. IMPACT OF DIRECT TAXES ON OPERATIONAL DECISIONS**

Operational decisions concern **day-to-day functioning, location, structure, innovation, and compliance**.

---

#### **(A) LOCATION AND EXPANSION DECISIONS**

**Concept:**
Companies choose locations based on **infrastructure, market access, labor, and tax environment**.

**Tax Impact:**
- **Special Economic Zones (SEZs)** offer:
  - 100% Income Tax exemption for first 5 years.
  - 50% exemption for next 5 years.
  - 50% on reinvested profits for next 5 years.
- **Tax holidays** for new manufacturing units (Section 115BAB) — corporate tax @ 15%.
- **State-specific incentives** (e.g., Gujarat, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu offer subsidies).
- **Northeast India** receives special tax exemptions.

**Implication:**
- Companies often **shift operations** to tax-favorable zones.
- Expansion decisions tied to **regional incentives**.

---

#### **(B) BUSINESS STRUCTURE & REORGANIZATION**

**Concept:**
The form of business (Proprietorship, Partnership, LLP, Company, Subsidiary) determines tax treatment.

**Tax Implications:**

| **Entity Type** | **Tax Rate** | **Special Features** |
|---|---|---|
| Proprietorship | Slab Rate | Simple compliance |
| Partnership / LLP | 30% flat | Partner's salary deductible |
| Company (Domestic) | 22% / 25% / 30% | Sec 115BAA / 115BAB benefits |
| New Manufacturing Co. | 15% (Sec 115BAB) | Reduced rate |
| Foreign Company | 35% + Surcharge | Higher rate |

**Implication:**
- Companies often **restructure** (mergers, demergers, slump sales) to **minimize tax liability**.
- Choice of structure depends on **scale, exit strategy, and tax efficiency**.

---

#### **(C) RESEARCH & DEVELOPMENT (R&D) AND INNOVATION**

**Concept:**
R&D drives innovation, competitiveness, and long-term growth.

**Tax Impact:**
- **Section 35** allows weighted deductions for R&D expenses.
- Special incentives for in-house R&D and approved scientific research associations.
- **Patent box regime** (Section 115BBF): Income from patents taxed @ 10%.

**Implication:**
- Encourages firms to invest in **innovation, technology, and patents**.
- Improves competitiveness in global markets.

---

#### **(D) OPERATIONAL EFFICIENCY & COMPLIANCE**

**Concept:**
Compliance with tax laws involves **filing returns, audits, documentation, and assessments**.

**Tax Impact:**
- **Complex tax laws** require significant administrative resources.
- Higher compliance costs can be a **barrier to growth for MSMEs**.
- **Penalties for non-compliance** affect operational stability.

**Implication:**
- Companies invest in **tax technology, ERP systems, and consultants**.
- Simplification of tax laws improves operational efficiency.

---

### **5. KEY DIRECT TAXES AFFECTING DECISIONS**

#### **(A) Corporate Income Tax (CIT):**
- Directly reduces **net profitability**.
- High rates correlate with **lower investment and growth**.
- Current Rates (FY 2025-26):
  - **22%** under Sec 115BAA (without exemptions)
  - **15%** under Sec 115BAB (new manufacturing companies)
  - **25%** for companies with turnover up to ₹400 Cr
  - **30%** for other domestic companies

#### **(B) Capital Gains Tax (CGT):**
- Influences the **timing of asset sales**.
- **Lower rates for long-term holdings** encourage long-term investment.
- LTCG: 12.5% (without indexation); 20% (with indexation for property).
- STCG: 20% (equity); slab rates (others).

#### **(C) Minimum Alternate Tax (MAT):**
- Ensures profitable companies pay **minimum tax** despite exemptions.
- Currently @ **15%** of book profits.
- Impacts cash flow of companies enjoying tax incentives.

#### **(D) Securities Transaction Tax (STT):**
- Levied on purchase/sale of securities on stock exchanges.
- Affects **trading and portfolio management** for companies.
- Rates vary: 0.1% on equity delivery; 0.025% on intraday sale; etc.

#### **(E) Dividend Tax:**
- Now taxed in **hands of shareholders** at slab rates.
- Affects **dividend distribution policy**.

---

### **6. EXAMPLES OF STRATEGIC DECISIONS DRIVEN BY TAX**

1. **Choice of Debt over Equity** — to avail tax shield.
2. **Setting up units in SEZs** — for tax holidays.
3. **Bonus shares instead of cash dividends** — to avoid dividend tax.
4. **Asset leasing instead of buying** — to claim full lease rentals as expense.
5. **R&D investments** — to claim weighted deductions.

---

### **7. DIFFERENCE BETWEEN DIRECT AND INDIRECT TAXES**

| **Parameter** | **Direct Tax** | **Indirect Tax** |
|---|---|---|
| **Imposition** | On income or profits | On goods and services |
| **Course of Payment** | Taxpayer pays directly | Through an intermediary |
| **Paying Entity** | Individuals and businesses | End consumers |
| **Tax Rate** | Decided based on income/profit | Same for everyone, based on goods/services |
| **Nature** | Progressive (rate increases with income) | Fixed/Regressive |
| **Burden Shifting** | Cannot be shifted | Can be shifted |
| **Examples** | Income Tax, Capital Gains Tax | GST, Customs Duty |
| **Impact on Inflation** | Less impact | Direct impact |
| **Equity** | Promotes equity | Less equitable |
| **Compliance** | Higher | Lower |

# **UNIT 2: STRUCTURE OF GST IN INDIA – CGST, SGST, UTGST, IGST**

---

### **1. INTRODUCTION TO GST**

The **Goods and Services Tax (GST)** is a **destination-based, dual-model consumption tax** introduced in India on **1st July 2017** by the Goods and Services Tax Act, 2017.

#### **Key Features:**
- **Single tax** replacing multiple indirect taxes (Excise, Service Tax, VAT, etc.).
- **Destination-based** — tax accrues to the state where goods/services are consumed.
- **Dual model** — both Centre and State levy tax simultaneously.
- **Comprehensive** — covers almost all goods and services.
- **One Nation, One Tax** — uniform tax structure across India.

#### **Constitutional Backing:**
- **101st Constitutional Amendment Act, 2016** introduced GST.
- Article 246A empowers both Centre and States to levy GST.
- **GST Council** (Article 279A) governs GST policy.

---

### **2. WHY DUAL GST MODEL?**

India is a **federal country** where both Centre and States have the power to levy taxes under the Constitution. To preserve **fiscal federalism**:

- Both **Central and State Governments** share GST revenue.
- A **three-tier tax structure** (CGST, SGST, IGST) was implemented.
- This ensures the principle of **"One Nation, One Tax"** while maintaining state autonomy.

---

### **3. KEY COMPONENTS OF GST STRUCTURE**

The GST structure in India has **four components**:

#### **(A) CGST – Central Goods and Services Tax**
- Levied by the **Central Government**.
- Applied on **intra-state transactions** (within the same state).
- Governed by the **CGST Act, 2017**.
- Revenue goes to the **Central Government**.

#### **(B) SGST – State Goods and Services Tax**
- Levied by the **State Government**.
- Applied on **intra-state transactions**.
- Governed by respective **State GST Acts**.
- Revenue goes to the **State Government**.

#### **(C) UTGST – Union Territory Goods and Services Tax**
- Levied by the **Union Territory Government** (without legislature).
- Applied on **intra-UT transactions**.
- Governed by the **UTGST Act, 2017**.
- Applies to: Ladakh, Andaman & Nicobar, Chandigarh, Dadra & Nagar Haveli and Daman & Diu, Lakshadweep.

#### **(D) IGST – Integrated Goods and Services Tax**
- Levied by the **Central Government**.
- Applied on **inter-state transactions, imports, and exports**.
- Governed by the **IGST Act, 2017**.
- Revenue is **shared** between Centre and consuming state.

---

### **4. STRUCTURE BREAKDOWN**

#### **(A) Intra-State Supply:**
- Sale within a state attracts **both CGST + SGST**.
- Equal split (e.g., 18% GST = 9% CGST + 9% SGST).

#### **(B) Inter-State Supply:**
- Sale outside the state attracts **IGST only** (e.g., 18%).
- Collected by Centre and shared with the consuming state.

#### **(C) Mechanism:**
- GST is **destination-based** — tax goes to the state of consumption, not production.

---

### **5. HOW TO DETERMINE THE TYPE OF GST?**

#### **(A) Intra-State Transactions:**
- Goods/services supplied within the same state or UT.
- **CGST + SGST/UTGST** is applicable.

#### **(B) Inter-State Transactions:**
- Goods/services supplied between two different states or UTs.
- **IGST** is applicable.

#### **(C) Imports / Exports:**
- IGST applies on imports.
- Exports are **zero-rated** (taxable at 0%).

#### **Importance of GSTIN:**
- **Goods and Services Tax Identification Number** is a 15-digit unique number.
- Helps identify whether transaction is **intra-state or inter-state**.
- Should be **validated** before issuing invoices.

---

### **6. APPLICABILITY OF VARIOUS GST TAXES**

#### **(A) IGST (Integrated GST):**

**Applicability:**
- Inter-state supplies.
- Imports into India.
- Exports from India (Zero-rated).
- Supplies to/from SEZ.

**Calculation Example:**
- M/s Rajesh Ltd (Chandigarh) sells goods to Anand Ltd (Dadra & Nagar Haveli) worth ₹1,00,000.
- GST Rate: 18% (IGST).
- IGST charged = ₹18,000.
- Goes to **Centre**, then shared with Dadra & Nagar Haveli (consuming state).

#### **(B) CGST (Central GST):**

**Applicability:**
- Intra-state supplies (within same state).
- Levied along with SGST.
- Maximum rate as per Sec 8 of CGST Act = **14%** (combined with SGST = 28%).

**ITC Rule:**
- CGST credit can be set off against **CGST or IGST** liability.
- Cannot be set off against SGST.

#### **(C) SGST (State GST):**

**Applicability:**
- Intra-state supplies.
- Levied along with CGST.
- Goes to the state government.

**Example:**
- M/s Rajesh Ltd (Chhattisgarh) sells to Vijay Ltd (Chhattisgarh) for ₹10,000.
- GST Rate: 18% (9% CGST + 9% SGST).
- Total tax = ₹1,800 (₹900 CGST + ₹900 SGST).
- ₹900 to Centre; ₹900 to Chhattisgarh Govt.

#### **(D) UTGST (Union Territory GST):**

**Applicability:**
- Supplies within Union Territories without legislature.
- Levied along with CGST.
- Governed by UTGST Act, 2017.

**Note:**
- UTs of **Delhi, J&K, and Puducherry** have legislatures → **SGST applies**.
- UTs of **Ladakh, Chandigarh, A&N, DNH&DD, Lakshadweep** → **UTGST applies**.

---

### **7. INPUT TAX CREDIT (ITC) – UTILIZATION RULES**

ITC allows businesses to **set off the tax paid on purchases** against tax payable on sales.

#### **Order of ITC Utilization:**

| **ITC Available** | **Can Be Used For** |
|---|---|
| **IGST Credit** | First IGST → Then CGST/SGST/UTGST (any order) |
| **CGST Credit** | First CGST → Then IGST (Cannot be used for SGST) |
| **SGST Credit** | First SGST → Then IGST (Cannot be used for CGST) |
| **UTGST Credit** | First UTGST → Then IGST (Cannot be used for CGST) |

#### **Important Rules:**
1. CGST and SGST credits **cannot be cross-utilized**.
2. IGST credit can be used for **any GST liability**.
3. ITC must be claimed within prescribed time limit.

---

### **8. GST RATE STRUCTURE (2025)**

GST is structured into multiple slab rates:

| **Slab** | **Application** |
|---|---|
| **0%** | Essential items (milk, fresh fruits, education) |
| **5%** | Common-use items (sugar, tea, edible oil) |
| **12%** | Intermediate items (processed foods) |
| **18%** | Standard rate (most goods/services) |
| **28%** | Luxury/sin goods (cars, tobacco) |
| **40%** | Newly introduced for specific luxury/sin goods |

**Note:** Late 2025 has seen rationalization to **5%, 18%, and 40%** as primary slabs.

---

### **9. KEY FACTS ABOUT GST**

1. **Destination-based Tax:** Tax accrues to the state where goods are consumed.
2. **Manufacturing State Loses Revenue:** Producing state does not benefit from GST on goods exported to other states.
3. **Centre acts as a Settlement Agency** for IGST.
4. **GST Council** decides rates and policies.
5. **Composition Scheme** is available for small businesses (turnover up to ₹1.5 Cr).
6. **Reverse Charge Mechanism (RCM)** applies in specific cases.

---

### **10. EXAMPLE OF GST CHAIN (Multiple Transactions)**

**Scenario:**
- A (Maharashtra) sells to B (Maharashtra) for ₹10,000 → CGST 9% + SGST 9%
- B (Maharashtra) sells to C (Rajasthan) for ₹17,500 → IGST 18%
- C (Rajasthan) sells to D (Rajasthan) for ₹30,000 → CGST 9% + SGST 9%

**Tax Flow:**
- Maharashtra collects SGST initially but transfers ₹900 to Centre (used for IGST payment).
- Centre transfers ₹450 IGST to Rajasthan (consuming state).
- Final tax of ₹2,700 each (CGST + SGST) accrues to Rajasthan and Centre.

**Conclusion:**
- Maharashtra (producing state) gets **NIL** revenue.
- Rajasthan (consuming state) gets **₹2,700** SGST.
- Centre gets **₹2,700** CGST.

---

### **11. PURPOSE OF THREE-TIER GST STRUCTURE**

The structure of CGST + SGST + IGST serves **two main purposes**:

#### **(A) One Nation, One Tax:**
- Unified tax structure across India.
- Eliminates cascading effect of taxes.
- Facilitates seamless ITC across states.

#### **(B) Dual Tax System:**
- Both Centre and States retain the right to levy tax.
- Preserves federal structure of the Constitution.
- Both governments earn revenue.

---

### **12. DIFFERENCE BETWEEN TYPES OF GST**

| **Type** | **Jurisdiction** | **Applicability** | **Authority Benefited** | **Priority of ITC Use** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **IGST** | Central Govt. | Inter-state, Imports | Central Govt. | IGST → CGST/SGST/UTGST (any) |
| **CGST** | Central Govt. | Intra-state, Intra-UT | Central Govt. | CGST → IGST |
| **SGST** | State Govt. | Intra-state | State Govt. | SGST → IGST |
| **UTGST** | UT Govt. | Intra-UT | UT Govt. | UTGST → IGST |

---

### **13. ADVANTAGES OF GST**

1. **Eliminates cascading effect** of taxes (Tax on Tax).
2. **Simplifies tax structure** across India.
3. **Boosts ease of doing business**.
4. **Wider tax base** and increased compliance.
5. **Promotes Make in India** initiative.
6. **Reduces logistic costs** (no entry tax, octroi).
7. **Uniform e-filing system** (GSTN portal).
8. **ITC mechanism** prevents double taxation.

---

### **14. CHALLENGES OF GST**

1. **Complex rate structure** (multiple slabs).
2. **Compliance burden** for small businesses.
3. **Frequent changes** in rates and procedures.
4. **Technical issues** on GSTN portal.
5. **Delayed refunds** for exporters.
6. **Reduced revenue for producing states**.

### **NUMERICAL 1: Tax Shield on Debt**

**Q.** ABC Ltd has Earnings Before Interest and Tax (EBIT) of ₹20,00,000. It has taken a loan of ₹50,00,000 @ 10%. Corporate tax rate is 30%. Calculate tax shield on debt.

**Solution:**

| **Particulars** | **Without Debt (₹)** | **With Debt (₹)** |
|---|---|---|
| EBIT | 20,00,000 | 20,00,000 |
| Less: Interest (50,00,000 × 10%) | NIL | (5,00,000) |
| Profit Before Tax | 20,00,000 | 15,00,000 |
| Less: Tax @ 30% | (6,00,000) | (4,50,000) |
| **Profit After Tax** | **14,00,000** | **10,50,000** |

**Tax Shield = Tax Saved = 6,00,000 – 4,50,000 = ₹1,50,000**

**Or simply: Interest × Tax Rate = 5,00,000 × 30% = ₹1,50,000**

---

### **NUMERICAL 2: Intra-State GST Calculation**

**Q.** XYZ Ltd in Maharashtra sells goods worth ₹2,50,000 to a customer in Maharashtra. GST rate is 18%. Calculate CGST and SGST.

**Solution:**

- Total GST = 18% × 2,50,000 = ₹45,000
- CGST = 9% × 2,50,000 = **₹22,500**
- SGST = 9% × 2,50,000 = **₹22,500**
- Invoice Value = 2,50,000 + 45,000 = **₹2,95,000**

**Distribution:**
- ₹22,500 to **Central Government**
- ₹22,500 to **Maharashtra State Government**

---

### **NUMERICAL 3: Inter-State GST Calculation**

**Q.** PQR Ltd in Gujarat sells goods worth ₹5,00,000 to a customer in Karnataka. GST rate is 18%. Calculate IGST.

**Solution:**

- IGST = 18% × 5,00,000 = **₹90,000**
- Invoice Value = 5,00,000 + 90,000 = **₹5,90,000**

**Distribution:**
- ₹90,000 collected by **Centre**
- Later, ₹45,000 (50%) transferred to **Karnataka** (consuming state).

### **NUMERICAL 4: ITC Utilization**

**Q.** A company has the following ITC and tax liabilities:
- ITC: IGST = ₹50,000; CGST = ₹30,000; SGST = ₹30,000
- Liability: IGST = ₹20,000; CGST = ₹40,000; SGST = ₹40,000

Calculate net tax payable.

**Solution:**

**Step 1: IGST Liability of ₹20,000**
- Use IGST Credit ₹20,000 → Liability NIL
- Remaining IGST Credit = ₹30,000

**Step 2: CGST Liability of ₹40,000**
- Use CGST Credit ₹30,000 → Liability ₹10,000
- Use IGST Credit ₹10,000 → Liability NIL
- Remaining IGST Credit = ₹20,000

**Step 3: SGST Liability of ₹40,000**
- Use SGST Credit ₹30,000 → Liability ₹10,000
- Use IGST Credit ₹10,000 → Liability NIL
- Remaining IGST Credit = ₹10,000

**Net Tax Payable = NIL**
**Carry Forward IGST Credit = ₹10,000**

### **NUMERICAL 5: Multi-Stage GST Chain**

**Q.** Manufacturer A (Maharashtra) sells goods to Dealer B (Maharashtra) for ₹10,000.
B sells to Trader C (Rajasthan) for ₹17,500.
C sells to End User D (Rajasthan) for ₹30,000.
GST rate: CGST 9%, SGST 9%, IGST 18%.

Calculate tax at each stage and final tax distribution.

**Solution:**

**Stage 1: A → B (Intra-state, Maharashtra)**
- Sale = ₹10,000
- CGST = ₹900; SGST = ₹900
- Total GST = ₹1,800

**Stage 2: B → C (Inter-state, Maharashtra → Rajasthan)**
- Sale = ₹17,500
- IGST = 17,500 × 18% = ₹3,150
- B's ITC: CGST ₹900 + SGST ₹900 = ₹1,800
- IGST to be paid in cash = 3,150 – 1,800 = ₹1,350
- (B uses CGST ₹900 and SGST ₹900 against IGST liability)

**Stage 3: C → D (Intra-state, Rajasthan)**
- Sale = ₹30,000
- CGST = ₹2,700; SGST = ₹2,700; Total = ₹5,400
- C's ITC: IGST ₹3,150
- After ITC: Total liability ₹5,400 – ₹3,150 = ₹2,250
- Suppose split: CGST liability = ₹2,700 – ₹1,575 (IGST credit) = ₹1,125
- SGST liability = ₹2,700 – ₹1,575 = ₹1,125

**Final Distribution:**
- Centre (CGST) gets eventual revenue of ~₹2,700.
- Rajasthan (SGST) gets eventual revenue of ~₹2,700.
- Maharashtra gets **NIL** (since goods were consumed in Rajasthan).

### **NUMERICAL 6: Comparison of Corporate Tax Regimes**

**Q.** ABC Ltd has profit of ₹1,00,00,000. Compare tax under:
(a) Section 115BAA (22% concessional)
(b) Normal Rate (30%)

**Solution:**

**Option (a) Sec 115BAA:**
- Tax = 1,00,00,000 × 22% = ₹22,00,000
- Surcharge @ 10% = ₹2,20,000
- Sub-total = ₹24,20,000
- Cess @ 4% = ₹96,800
- **Total Tax = ₹25,16,800**

**Option (b) Normal Rate (30%):**
- Tax = 1,00,00,000 × 30% = ₹30,00,000
- Surcharge @ 7% = ₹2,10,000
- Sub-total = ₹32,10,000
- Cess @ 4% = ₹1,28,400
- **Total Tax = ₹33,38,400**

**Tax Saved (115BAA) = 33,38,400 – 25,16,800 = ₹8,21,600**

**Conclusion:** Section 115BAA is more beneficial.

### **NUMERICAL 7: GST Computation with ITC**

**Q.** A trader purchases goods worth ₹1,00,000 (GST @ 18% extra). He sells at ₹1,50,000 (GST @ 18%). Calculate net GST liability.

**Solution:**

- **Output GST** = 1,50,000 × 18% = ₹27,000
- **Input GST (ITC)** = 1,00,000 × 18% = ₹18,000
- **Net GST Payable** = 27,000 – 18,000 = **₹9,000**

## **KEY TAKEAWAYS FOR EXAM PREPARATION**

### **Direct Taxes & Financial Decisions:**

1. **Master Tax Shield Concept:** Interest on debt is tax-deductible → reduces tax liability.
2. **Capital Structure:** Companies prefer debt for tax efficiency, balanced against financial risk.
3. **Dividend Policy:** Influenced by dividend tax regime (now in shareholders' hands).
4. **Investment Decisions:** Driven by depreciation rules, R&D incentives, SEZ benefits.
5. **Operational Decisions:** Location (SEZ/Northeast), Structure (LLP/Co.), R&D, Compliance.
6. **Key Rates:**
   - Sec 115BAA = 22% (without exemptions)
   - Sec 115BAB = 15% (new manufacturing)
   - MAT = 15%
   - LTCG = 12.5% (without indexation)

### **GST Structure:**

1. **Four Components:** CGST, SGST, UTGST, IGST.
2. **Intra-state:** CGST + SGST (or UTGST).
3. **Inter-state / Imports:** IGST.
4. **Destination-based:** Tax goes to consuming state.
5. **ITC Rules:**
   - IGST Credit → IGST → CGST/SGST/UTGST.
   - CGST Credit → CGST → IGST (NOT SGST).
   - SGST Credit → SGST → IGST (NOT CGST).
6. **Standard GST Rates:** 0%, 5%, 12%, 18%, 28%, 40%.
7. **Constitutional Backing:** 101st Amendment, Articles 246A & 279A.
8. **Purpose:** "One Nation, One Tax" + Dual Tax System.

### **Strategic Memorization Tips:**

1. **Tax Sections:** 115BAA, 115BAB, 115BBF, 35, 32, 80G.
2. **GST Acts:** CGST Act 2017, IGST Act 2017, UTGST Act 2017.
3. **Examples:** Always remember concrete tax computation examples.
4. **Comparison Tables:** Direct vs. Indirect, Old vs. New Regime, GST types.
5. **Numerical Practice:** Tax shield, ITC utilization, GST chain calculations.

### **Exam Strategy:**

1. **Theory:** Use diagrams, tabular formats, and section references.
2. **Numericals:** Show all working notes; use proper computation format.
3. **MCQs:** Focus on dates, sections, rates, and key concepts.
4. **Time Management:** Allocate based on marks distribution.
5. **Cross-referencing:** Link concepts (e.g., tax shield → capital structure → financial decision).
