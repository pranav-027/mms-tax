# **UNIT 1: SCOPE OF TAXATION & RESIDENTIAL STATUS**

---

### **1. SCOPE OF TAXATION**

The scope of taxation refers to the **extent of the government's authority** to impose and collect taxes from individuals, businesses, activities, and assets within its jurisdiction. Taxation is a sovereign power essential for funding public services and infrastructure.

#### **Key Aspects of the Scope of Taxation:**

**(i) Comprehensive Coverage:**
The power of taxation extends over:
- Persons (residents and non-residents)
- Property (movable and immovable)
- Businesses and professions
- Activities, rights, and privileges existing in the country.

**(ii) Constitutional Basis:**
- Authority is derived from the **Indian Constitution**.
- Empowers the legislature to enact and enforce tax laws.
- Subject to constitutional limitations (due process, equal protection, etc.).

**(iii) Types of Taxes Covered:**
- **Direct Taxes** – Income Tax, Wealth Tax (on income, wealth, profits).
- **Indirect Taxes** – GST, Customs Duty (on goods and services).

**(iv) Public Purpose:**
Taxes must be levied for a public purpose (welfare, infrastructure, defence, etc.).

**(v) Administrative Functions:**
The Income Tax Department (executive branch) handles assessment, collection, and enforcement.

---

### **2. CONCEPT OF RESIDENTIAL STATUS**

The **residential status** of an assessee determines the scope of his taxable income in India. The Income Tax Department uses residential status as a key factor to decide tax liability — particularly the extent to which global income is taxable.

#### **Categories of Residential Status (Individuals):**

```
                    Assessee
                       |
        ----------------------------------
        |                                |
     Resident                       Non-Resident (NR)
        |
   --------------
   |            |
  ROR         RNOR
(Resident &  (Resident but Not
Ordinarily   Ordinarily Resident)
Resident)
```

---

### **3. RESIDENT – BASIC CONDITIONS [Section 6(1)]**

An individual is a **Resident** of India if he satisfies **any one** of the following two conditions:

**Condition 1:** Stay in India for **182 days or more** during the previous year, **OR**

**Condition 2:** Stay in India for **60 days or more** during the previous year **AND** **365 days or more** during the **4 immediately preceding years**.

---

### **4. EXCEPTIONS TO RESIDENTIAL STATUS**

**Exception 1:** An Indian citizen who **leaves India** during the FY:
- As a member of the crew of an Indian ship, **OR**
- For the purpose of employment abroad

→ Will be a Resident **only if** stay in India is **182 days or more**.
(60-day rule is not applicable.)

**Exception 2:** An Indian citizen or Person of Indian Origin (PIO) who comes to India on a **visit**:
- If total income (excluding foreign income) **exceeds ₹15 lakh**:
  - 60-day condition is replaced by **120 days**.
- If total income (excluding foreign income) is **up to ₹15 lakh**:
  - 60-day condition is replaced by **182 days** only.

---

### **5. DEEMED RESIDENT (Significant Amendment from FY 2020-21)**

An individual will be treated as a **"Deemed Resident of India"** if:
- He is a **citizen of India**,
- His total income (other than from foreign sources) **exceeds ₹15 lakh**, AND
- He is **not liable to tax in any other country** (NIL tax liability abroad).

A Deemed Resident is, by default, treated as **RNOR**.

---

### **6. RESIDENT AND ORDINARILY RESIDENT (ROR)**

A Resident becomes **ROR** if he satisfies **BOTH** the following additional conditions:

**Condition 1:** Has been a **Resident in India for at least 2 out of 10** immediately preceding previous years, **AND**

**Condition 2:** Has stayed in India for **at least 730 days** during the **7 immediately preceding** previous years.

---

### **7. RESIDENT BUT NOT ORDINARILY RESIDENT (RNOR)**

An individual is **RNOR** in any of the following 3 situations:

1. He is a Resident, but fails to satisfy **either or both** of the additional ROR conditions.
2. Indian citizen / PIO with income > ₹15 lakh (excl. foreign income), staying in India for **120 days or more but less than 182 days**.
3. He is a **Deemed Resident** under the amendment.

---

### **8. NON-RESIDENT (NR)**

An individual who **fails to satisfy both** basic conditions of Section 6(1):
- Stay in India < 182 days in P.Y., **AND**
- Stay in India < 60 days in P.Y. (or 60+ days but < 365 days in preceding 4 years)

→ Is treated as a **Non-Resident** for that financial year.

---

### **9. POINTS TO NOTE WHILE COUNTING DAYS OF STAY**

1. Stay in India includes stay in **territorial waters** (12 nautical miles from coastline).
2. The period of stay **need not be continuous or active**.
3. **Both date of arrival and date of departure** are counted as stay in India.
4. Residential status has **NO connection** with citizenship, place of birth, or domicile.
5. A person can be a resident in **more than one country** simultaneously.

---

### **10. IMPORTANT TERMINOLOGIES**

| **Term** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| **Income from Foreign Sources** | Income earned outside India (excluding business controlled / profession set up in India) |
| **Non-Resident Indian (NRI)** | Citizen of India / Indian origin but not a resident |
| **Person of Indian Origin (PIO)** | A person whose self, parents, or grandparents were born in undivided India |

---

### **11. TAXABILITY (INCIDENCE OF TAX) – Section 5**

| **Type of Income** | **ROR** | **RNOR** | **NR** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Income received / deemed to be received in India | Taxable | Taxable | Taxable |
| Income accrued / deemed to accrue in India | Taxable | Taxable | Taxable |
| Income accrued and received outside India from a business **controlled from India** | Taxable | Taxable | Not Taxable |
| Income accrued and received outside India from any other source | Taxable | **Not Taxable** | Not Taxable |
| Past untaxed income brought into India | Not Taxable | Not Taxable | Not Taxable |

**Key Summary:**
- **ROR** = Taxed on **Global Income** (worldwide).
- **RNOR** = Taxed on Indian income + Foreign income from business controlled in India.
- **NR** = Taxed only on income **received or accrued in India**.

---

### **12. TAX INCIDENCE**

- Tax incidence refers to the **person who ultimately bears the tax burden**.
- Even if tax is remitted by another person, liability rests on the actual income earner.
- The person on whom incidence falls is the one who effectively bears the cost.

**Examples:**
- *Received in India* → Interest on Fixed Deposits in Indian banks.
- *Received from India* → Payment from an Indian company into a foreign bank account for services rendered.

# **UNIT 2: EXCLUSIONS / EXEMPTIONS FROM TOTAL INCOME (SECTION 10)**

---

### **1. MEANING OF EXCLUSIONS**

**Exclusions from Total Income** are incomes that are **not included** while computing the total taxable income of an assessee. These are also called **"Exempt Incomes"** and are mostly governed by **Section 10** of the Income Tax Act, 1961.

---

### **2. COMMON EXEMPTIONS UNDER SECTION 10**

| **Exemption** | **Description** |
|---|---|
| **Agricultural Income** | Income from agricultural land in India |
| **House Rent Allowance (HRA)** | Exempt subject to specific formula (lower of 3) |
| **Leave Travel Allowance (LTA)** | Exempt for travel within India |
| **Gratuity & Pension** | Specific limits for govt./private employment |
| **Life Insurance Proceeds** | Sum assured + bonus (subject to Sec 10(10D)) |
| **PPF Interest** | Interest on Provident Fund is fully exempt |
| **Scholarships** | Granted to meet cost of education |
| **HUF Income** | Sum received as a member of HUF from family income |
| **Retirement Benefits** | Leave encashment, gratuity, commuted pension |

---

### **3. KEY CONSIDERATIONS**

1. **Old vs New Regime:** Many exemptions (HRA, LTA, etc.) are **only available under the Old Regime**.
2. **Mandatory Disclosure:** Even exempt incomes must be disclosed in the ITR.
3. **Minimum Wage Earners:** In some jurisdictions, minimum wages (incl. holiday/overtime pay) are excluded.
4. **Employee Benefits:** Employer-provided health insurance & specific allowances may be exempt.

### **7. EXEMPTION ON LIFE INSURANCE POLICY – Section 10(10D)**

Income (including bonus) from a Life Insurance Policy is **exempt** under this section.

#### **Exclusions (Where Section 10(10D) Does NOT Apply):**

1. **Keyman Insurance Policy** taken by employer on employee's life.
2. Policy on the life of a **specially-abled dependent family member** (covered under different sections).
3. Policies where **premium exceeds 10% of sum assured**.

---

### **8. EXEMPTION ON HOUSE RENT ALLOWANCE – Section 10(13A)**

HRA is the part of salary received by an employee to cover **rent and accommodation expenses**.

#### **Amount of Exemption – Rule 2A**

Exempt amount = **LEAST of the following three**:

1. **Actual HRA received** from employer.
2. **50% of (Basic Salary + DA)** if living in Delhi, Mumbai, Chennai, or Kolkata
   **OR** **40% of (Basic Salary + DA)** if living in any other city.
3. **Actual Rent Paid – 10% of (Basic Salary + DA)**.

#### **Expenses Covered Under HRA:**

1. **Actual rent paid** for residential accommodation.
2. **Brokerage / commission** to a real estate agent.
3. **Maintenance charges** (society fees, common facility charges).
4. **Lease agreement costs** (preparation & registration).

**Note:** Only expenses **directly related** to rented accommodation are eligible.

# **UNIT 3: INCOME TAX SLABS – FY 2025-26 (AY 2026-27)**

---

### **1. NEW TAX REGIME – DEFAULT REGIME**

Under Budget 2025, slab rates have been significantly relaxed.

| **Income Slab (₹)** | **Tax Rate** |
|---|---|
| Up to 4,00,000 | NIL |
| 4,00,001 – 8,00,000 | 5% |
| 8,00,001 – 12,00,000 | 10% |
| 12,00,001 – 16,00,000 | 15% |
| 16,00,001 – 20,00,000 | 20% |
| 20,00,001 – 24,00,000 | 25% |
| Above 24,00,000 | 30% |

**Key Highlights:**
- **Rebate u/s 87A** = ₹60,000 → income up to ₹12,00,000 effectively tax-free.
- **Standard Deduction** = ₹75,000 → effective tax-free salary up to **₹12,75,000**.
- **No** concessional slab for senior citizens.
- Rebate **NOT applicable** on capital gains, crypto, online gaming income.

---

### **2. OLD TAX REGIME (UNCHANGED)**

#### **A. Individuals < 60 Years, NRIs, HUF**

| 5,00,001 – 10,00,000 | 20% |
| Above 10,00,000 | 30% |

#### **B. Senior Citizens (60–80 Years)**

| **Income Slab (₹)** | **Tax Rate** |
|---|---|

#### **C. Super Senior Citizens (Above 80 Years)**

| **Income Slab (₹)** | **Tax Rate** |
|---|---|
| Up to 5,00,000 | NIL |

| Above 10,00,000 | 30% |

**Old Regime Deductions Available:** 80C, 80D, 80G, 80TTA, HRA, LTA, Section 24 (home loan interest), 80E (education loan), etc.

---

### **3. SURCHARGE RATES**

| **Income Range** | **Old Regime** | **New Regime** |
|---|---|---|
| ₹50 lakh – ₹1 crore | 10% | 10% |
| ₹1 crore – ₹2 crore | 15% | 15% |
| ₹2 crore – ₹5 crore | 25% | 25% |
| Above ₹5 crore | 37% | 25% (capped) |

**Health & Education Cess** = **4%** on (Tax + Surcharge) under both regimes.

---

### **4. TAX-FREE INCOME LIMIT (FY 2025-26)**

| **Regime** | **Tax-Free Limit** |
|---|---|
| New Regime (Salaried) | ₹12,75,000 (₹12 lakh + ₹75,000 standard deduction) |
| New Regime (Non-salaried) | ₹12,00,000 |
| Old Regime | ₹5,00,000 |

---

### **5. NEW vs OLD REGIME – DIFFERENCES**

| **Basis** | **Old Regime** | **New Regime** |
|---|---|---|
| Deductions/Exemptions | Wide range (80C, HRA, etc.) | Very limited |
| Beneficial for | Taxpayers with high deductions | Middle-income earners with no investments |
| Default Regime | Optional (need to opt-in) | Default |
| Standard Deduction | ₹50,000 | ₹75,000 |
| Maximum Rebate u/s 87A | ₹12,500 | ₹60,000 |

---

### **6. BREAK-EVEN DEDUCTIONS (FOR REGIME SELECTION)**

If actual deductions **exceed** break-even, choose **Old Regime**.

| **Gross Income (₹)** | **Break-even Deductions (₹)** |
|---|---|
| Up to 5 lakh | Both regimes equally beneficial |
| 7 lakh | 1,50,000 |
| 10 lakh | 4,50,000 |
| 12 lakh | 6,50,000 |
| 15 lakh | 5,43,750 |
| 20 lakh | 7,08,330 |
| 25 lakh | 8,00,000 |

---

### **7. SHIFTING REGIME – FORM 10-IEA**

- New Regime is the **default**. To opt for Old Regime → file **Form 10-IEA**.
- Form 10-IEA is mandatory **only for taxpayers with business income** opting in/out of Old Regime.
- Salaried individuals can switch every year directly through ITR.

### **Numerical 1: Determining Residential Status**

**Q.** Mr. Raj, an Indian citizen, left India on 1st October 2024 for the first time for employment abroad. Determine his residential status for FY 2024-25.

**Solution:**
- Stay in India during P.Y. 2024-25 = 1st April 2024 to 30th Sept 2024 = **183 days**.
- Since he left India for employment, **only 182-day rule** applies (Exception 1).
- Stay = 183 days ≥ 182 days → **Resident**.
- He must satisfy ROR conditions to be ROR; otherwise, RNOR.

---

### **Numerical 2: PIO Visiting India**

**Q.** Mr. Sam, a PIO, came to India on a visit on 1st November 2024 and left on 31st March 2025. His income (excluding foreign source) is ₹20 lakh. Determine status.

**Solution:**
- Stay in India = 1st Nov 2024 to 31st March 2025 = **151 days**.
- Income > ₹15 lakh → 60-day condition replaced by **120 days**.
- Stay = 151 days ≥ 120 days → Check 365 days in preceding 4 years.
- Assuming he was in India for 365+ days in preceding 4 years → **Resident**.
- Since stay is between 120–182 days with income > ₹15 lakh → **RNOR**.

### **Numerical 3: HRA Exemption Calculation**

**Q.** Mr. Akash works in Mumbai. His basic salary is ₹40,000 p.m., DA (forming part of salary) ₹10,000 p.m., HRA received ₹20,000 p.m., and rent paid is ₹18,000 p.m. Calculate the exempt HRA.

**Solution:**

Annual figures:
- Basic + DA = (40,000 + 10,000) × 12 = ₹6,00,000
- HRA Received = 20,000 × 12 = ₹2,40,000
- Rent Paid = 18,000 × 12 = ₹2,16,000

Exemption is **LEAST** of:
1. Actual HRA = ₹2,40,000
2. 50% of (Basic + DA) [Mumbai] = ₹3,00,000
3. Rent paid – 10% of (Basic + DA) = 2,16,000 – 60,000 = **₹1,56,000**

**HRA Exempt = ₹1,56,000**
**Taxable HRA = 2,40,000 – 1,56,000 = ₹84,000**

### **Numerical 4: Tax Liability under New Regime**

**Q.** Mr. Verma has a salary income of ₹15,00,000 for FY 2025-26. Calculate his tax liability under the New Regime.

**Solution:**

| Particulars | Amount (₹) |
|---|---|
| Gross Salary | 15,00,000 |
| Less: Standard Deduction | (75,000) |
| **Taxable Income** | **14,25,000** |

**Tax Computation (New Regime):**
- Up to ₹4,00,000 → NIL = 0
- ₹4,00,001 – ₹8,00,000 (5% × 4,00,000) = 20,000
- ₹8,00,001 – ₹12,00,000 (10% × 4,00,000) = 40,000
- ₹12,00,001 – ₹14,25,000 (15% × 2,25,000) = 33,750

**Total Tax = ₹93,750**
- Add: Health & Education Cess @ 4% = ₹3,750

**Net Tax Payable = ₹97,500**

### **Numerical 6: Comparing Old vs New Regime**

**Q.** Mr. Mehta has gross income of ₹12,00,000 with deductions of ₹2,00,000 (80C, 80D). Compare tax liability.

**Solution:**

**Old Regime:**
- Taxable Income = 12,00,000 – 50,000 (Std. Ded.) – 2,00,000 = ₹9,50,000
- Tax = 12,500 + 20% × (9,50,000 – 5,00,000) = 12,500 + 90,000 = **₹1,02,500**
- Cess @ 4% = ₹4,100
- **Total = ₹1,06,600**

**New Regime:**
- Taxable Income = 12,00,000 – 75,000 = ₹11,25,000
- Income ≤ ₹12,00,000 → Eligible for **Rebate u/s 87A (₹60,000)**
- **Net Tax Payable = NIL**

**Conclusion:** **New Regime** is more beneficial – saves ₹1,06,600.

### **Numerical 8: Calculation of LTA Exemption**

### **Numerical 9: Surcharge Calculation**

**Tax = ₹40,80,000**

7. **Standard Deduction:** ₹50,000 (Old) vs. ₹75,000 (New).

8. **Form 10-IEA** is required to opt out of New Regime if having business income.

10. **Practice numerical questions** repeatedly – HRA, tax slabs, residential status, regime comparison.
